Machine Learning Notes
 
## What Is Machine Learning
Machine learning is a way of building models that learn patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules. A model is trained on examples and then makes predictions on new data.
 
## Types of Learning
Supervised learning uses labeled data. Examples are classification (predict a category, such as spam or not spam) and regression (predict a number, such as a house price). Unsupervised learning finds structure in unlabeled data, such as clustering and dimensionality reduction. Reinforcement learning trains an agent to maximize reward through trial and error.
 
## Common Algorithms
Linear regression predicts a number from a weighted sum of features. Logistic regression predicts class probabilities. Decision trees split data by feature thresholds. Random forests average many trees to reduce overfitting. Gradient boosting builds trees one after another, each fixing the errors of the last. K-means groups points into k clusters. Neural networks stack layers of weighted sums and non-linear activations.
 
## Training Workflow
1. Collect and clean the data. 2. Split it into training, validation and test sets. 3. Choose features and a model. 4. Train the model on the training set. 5. Tune hyperparameters on the validation set. 6. Evaluate once on the test set. Never tune on the test set, or the score will be too optimistic.
 
## Overfitting and Underfitting
Overfitting means the model memorizes the training data and performs badly on new data (low training error, high test error). Underfitting means the model is too simple to capture the pattern (high error on both). Remedies for overfitting: more data, simpler model, regularization (L1 or L2), dropout, early stopping, cross-validation.
 
## Bias-Variance Tradeoff
Bias is error from overly simple assumptions. Variance is error from sensitivity to the particular training set. Making a model more complex lowers bias but raises variance. The goal is the balance with the lowest total error.
 
## Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy is the share of correct predictions, and it misleads on imbalanced data. Precision is the share of predicted positives that are correct. Recall is the share of actual positives that were found. F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. ROC-AUC measures how well the model ranks positives above negatives. For regression, use MAE, MSE, RMSE and R-squared.
 
## Gradient Descent
Gradient descent minimizes a loss function by repeatedly moving the parameters a small step opposite to the gradient. The step size is the learning rate: too large and training diverges, too small and it is slow. Stochastic and mini-batch gradient descent use a subset of the data per step.
 
## Feature Engineering
Scale numeric features (standardization or min-max) for models sensitive to scale, such as k-NN, SVM and neural networks. Encode categories with one-hot encoding. Handle missing values by imputing or flagging them. Avoid data leakage: never let information from the test set or the future influence training.
 
## Embeddings and Retrieval
An embedding maps text, images or other items to a vector so that similar items are close together. Cosine similarity compares embeddings. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) retrieves relevant chunks of documents by embedding similarity and gives them to a language model as context, so answers are grounded in the documents.
